Silkworms apart from being the primary source of natural silk; also serve as a powerful model system for advancing our understanding of insect biology, genetics, and host–pathogen interactions. My research expertise lies at the intersection of computational genomics and applied biotechnology, with a particular focus on integrating advanced sequencing technologies, molecular tools, and bioinformatics to address both fundamental and applied questions in sericulture and biotechnology.

**Genomics-Assisted Breeding of Samia ricini**

A key focus of my work is the application of advanced sequencing technologies and computational genomics to the improvement of *Samia ricini*, a commercially important silkworm species. Through the use of genome-wide association studies, marker-assisted selection, and functional genomics, my research seeks to identify genetic determinants of economically valuable traits.

This genomics-driven breeding approach enables the development of silkworm strains, ensuring greater productivity and sustainability in the sericulture industry.

**Silkworm–Virus Interaction Systems for Biotechnology**

My lab also investigates the silkworm–virus interaction system as a platform for innovation in biotechnology. By studying the molecular basis of host–virus dynamics, we aim to harness baculoviruses and related systems for heterologous protein expression and therapeutic applications. Engineer silkworm-based expression systems as efficient, scalable platforms for biopharming. This area of research bridges fundamental virology and applied biotechnology, creating opportunities for novel industrial and biomedical applications.

**Germplasm Management of *Morus* spp.**

Silkworm productivity is closely linked to the quality of its host plant, *Morus* spp. (mulberry). My research extends to the genetic characterization and bioresource management of mulberry germplasm, applying molecular markers, genome sequencing, and computational tools to: **a)** Assess genetic diversity across germplasm collections **b)** Develop genomic resources for Mulberry conservation **c)** Enable precision management of mulberry resources for improved sericulture outcomes

This integrated work ensures that silkworm and mulberry breeding programs are aligned, sustainable, and backed by genomic insights.

**Broader Research Contributions**

By combining advanced genomic technologies, computational biology, and applied biotechnology, my research contributes to three interconnected domains:

- Genomics-assisted silkworm breeding for commercial and sustainable sericulture

- Silkworm–virus systems as tools for developing novel biotechnologies

- Genomic management of mulberry germplasm as a foundation for enhanced silkworm productivity

This holistic approach strengthens both the scientific understanding and industrial applications of silkworm biology, ultimately advancing innovations in genomics, bioresource management, and biotechnology.
